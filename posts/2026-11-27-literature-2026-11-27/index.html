Latest2026-11-27 · Beijing

Cross-platform alignment is distinct from correction of protein-specific bias. Instrument-specific learning improves de novo peptide sequencing from timsTOF spectra

Michael Dammann; Bhavya Krishnan; Sander Willems; Justin Sanders; Fabian J. Theis; Wout Bittremieux; George Rosenberger; William Stafford Noble

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-25
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.19.767847
Publication status:
Preprint

The authors trained a timsTOF-specific Casanovo model on nearly seven million peptide–spectrum matches. On held-out timsTOF data, prediction coverage was more than four times that of the strongest Orbitrap-trained baseline at 90% precision and more than eight times at 95%. The HLA-I application recovered 15% more unique peptides than database search alone, but the 280 noncanonical candidates were not independently confirmed by that comparison.

Reference-Anchored Scale Alignment Enables Cross-platform Harmonization of Protein Concentrations in Label-Free Plasma Proteomics

André Q. Figueiredo; Inês F. Domingos; Luís B. Carvalho; Jacek R. Wiśniewski; Rajiv Dhir; Carlos Lodeiro; José L. Capelo; Dimitrios Korentzelos; Hugo M. Santos

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-11-26
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c01595
Publication status:
Journal article

RAAP rescales concentrations from the total protein approach using an endogenous reference protein. Across 330 LC–MS/MS runs from 22 configurations in eight labs, anchoring to SERPINA1 lowered the upper 95% limit of agreement from 3.7-fold to 2.8-fold, whereas albumin made agreement worse. The transferrin-based gain in reference-interval concordance for lower-coverage runs was not statistically significant, and global rescaling does not correct protein-specific biases.

iMATE: Individualized Metabolite Association Topology Events for Detecting Metabolic Remodeling in Colorectal Cancer

Shuang Gao; Lingli Deng; Jingjing Xu; Zhaodong Fei; Daniel Raftery; Jiyang Dong

Journal of Proteome Research · 2026

First online:
2026-11-26
DOI:
10.1021/acs.jproteome.6c00841
Publication status:
Journal article

iMATE constructs individual metabolite association networks against a common healthy reference and uses zero-dimensional persistent homology to track when metabolites join the network and when connected components merge. In serum from 66 cases and 92 controls, it identified 26 topology-associated metabolites, 12 not detected by differential abundance analysis. Simulations and three stool datasets illustrate applicability, but network edges are not causal evidence and the study does not establish external diagnostic performance.

Spatial-spectral hypergraph attention for unsupervised segmentation of lung cancer MALDI-FTICR imaging: a leave-one-patient-out benchmark against classical clustering

Amine Bougarne

Research Square · 2026

First online:
2026-11-25
DOI:
10.21203/rs.3.rs-11327285/v1
Publication status:
Preprint (v1)

This MALDI-FTICR imaging study compares spatial–spectral hypergraph attention with classical clustering under leave-one-patient-out validation. K-means on standardized spectra gave a higher silhouette in all seven held-out patients (mean 0.445 versus 0.297), but the differences were not significant after Holm correction. The small cohort does not establish general superiority; it does make a case for patient-level validation and a simple baseline.

MaldiSuite: a Python ecosystem for MALDI-TOF spectral processing and analysis in antimicrobial resistance research

Ettore Rocchi; Claudia Sala; Gastone Castellani

Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-11-21
DOI:
10.1093/bioinformatics/btag848
Publication status:
Accepted manuscript

MaldiSuite unifies MALDI-TOF spectral preprocessing, drift monitoring, multisite batch correction, and classification behind a scikit-learn interface, separating fitting from transformation to prevent cross-validation leakage. The authors report AUROC up to 0.92 for the DRIAMS-A S. aureus/oxacillin task and illustrate the optimistic bias caused by leakage. That single-task maximum does not establish transfer across sites or resistance phenotypes.

Search-software choice shapes the discoverable androgen receptor proximal interactome in DIA proteomics

Lilliana Radoshevich; Michael E. E. Wright

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-25
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.15.766752
Publication status:
Preprint

On the same timsTOF Pro2 dia-PASEF data with cross-run transfer disabled in both workflows, Spectronaut 21 identified 24–38% more proteins, whereas FragPipe 24.0 had biological-replicate CVs 4.1–6.4 percentage points lower. Each workflow still used its own library, so this single proximity-labeling dataset compares complete workflows rather than isolating search-engine effects or establishing ground truth.

Two-Dimensional Correlation Mass Spectrometry for Precursor/Fragment Ion Identification of Composite and Unresolved Spectral Peaks

Elly A. Breves; Garon H. Lowke; Jacob T. Shelley

Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry · 2026

First online:
2026-11-13
DOI:
10.1021/jasms.6c00308
Publication status:
Journal article

Two-dimensional correlation mass spectrometry uses cross-correlation to estimate the contributions of atenolol and metoprolol to shared fragments and to assign regions of an unresolved isotope/protonated-ion overlap. Across mixture ratios, calibration was comparable to peak fitting while adding evidence for peak origin. The study did not establish performance for unknowns or complex mixtures.

NovoTax: sample-specific database construction for prokaryotic mass spectrometry-based proteomics

Dennis Svedberg; André Mateus

Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-11-24
DOI:
10.1093/bioinformatics/btag860
Publication status:
Accepted manuscript

NovoTax uses de novo peptide sequences from bottom-up spectra to search a large reference-proteome collection, then builds a compact sample-specific database for conventional searches. On isolates it usually recovered the reported species and strain neighborhood, and in mixtures it recovered abundant members, not a complete inventory of rare organisms. The core method previously appeared as an April preprint.

MSRyzen: An Agentic Platform for Unified Proteomics Analysis and Evidence-Centric Visualization

Yucheng Liao; Jubao Cheng; Zexu Xiao; Tao Yi; Jiaxiang Ding; Weijie Zhang

arXiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-20
DOI:
10.48550/arXiv.2611.27246
Publication status:
Preprint (v1)

MSRyzen turns natural-language requests into workflow graphs and connects parameter tuning with peak-level and sequence-level evidence inspection. Across four public top-down datasets it reported 3.6–15.8% more identifications than defaults at a nominal proteoform FDR of 1%. The tuning objective uses identification counts on the data being tuned, so this is neither an independent gain estimate nor a validation of realized FDR.
