Literature Brief (November 10, 2026)

Latest2026-11-10 · Beijing
English中文

This brief examines a computational framework for analyzing processed single-cell proteomics data. Cell clustering and protein-module recovery provide complementary views, but neither alone establishes a biological mechanism or a physical protein complex.

scYOU: a multi-scale yoked optimization and unsupervised framework for analysis of cellular heterogeneity and complex-associated protein module recovery

Yuan Gan; Yixiang Huang; Xinqi Gong

Briefings in Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-11-09
DOI:
10.1093/bib/bbag626
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article

scYOU couples cell-level and protein-level autoencoder representations, using supercell alignment and Gene Ontology cellular-component priors to connect cellular heterogeneity with protein modules. The study evaluates six mass-spectrometry-based single-cell protein-abundance datasets, making it relevant to downstream proteomics analysis rather than raw-spectrum processing. Joint representations may help organize candidate cell states and complex-associated modules, but the results depend on data preprocessing and the supplied biological priors. Coherent protein modules should therefore be treated as hypotheses for follow-up, not direct evidence of physical complexes or causal mechanisms.
