Limitations of de novo peptide sequencing in resolving sequence ambiguity

Sam van Puyenbroeck; Denis Beslic; Tomi Suomi; Tanja Holstein; Thilo Muth; Laura L. Elo; Lennart Martens; Robbin Bouwmeester; Tim Van Den Bossche; Tine Claeys

Nature Methods · 2026

First online:
2026-11-03
DOI:
10.1038/s41592-026-03301-9
Publication status:
Journal article

A comparison of eight de novo sequencing models and three post-processing approaches finds that many shared errors arise from insufficient fragment-ion coverage, with limited gains from post-processing. This motivates explicit uncertainty handling rather than treating every full-sequence prediction as resolved. Conclusions depend on the selected datasets and database-search reference identifications.

Reading scope: Publisher-indexed abstract and selected results and methods; no reproduction.

ProteoAnalyst: Connecting biological context and peptide-level evidence in quantitative proteomics and phosphoproteomics

Guangyan Zhou; Gordon Li; Danielle M. Simons; Alyssa Weinrauch; Nolan Sammut; Jelena Kolic; Haoning Howard Cen; Daina Avizonis; Peter Siegel; Francis C. Lynn; Patrick E. MacDonald; James D. Johnson; Jean-François Trempe; Jianguo Xia

Protein Science · 2026

First online:
2026-11-12
DOI:
10.1002/pro.70851
Publication status:
Journal article

ProteoAnalyst connects statistical analysis, pathway and network interpretation with traceable peptide-level evidence. Across five benchmarks no workflow won uniformly; in HYE124, MSstats had an empirical FDR of 0.063 versus 0.100 for the default DIA workflow. Relative modification abundance must not be interpreted as modification occupancy.

Reading scope: Selected abstract, methods, results and discussion; supplements not read and no reproduction.

A unified and practical taxonomy for false discovery rate control in omics research

Thomas Burger; Xinzhou Ge; Jingyi Jessica Li

Nature Biotechnology · 2026

First online:
2026-11-13
DOI:
10.1038/s41587-026-03379-8
Publication status:
Comment

This Comment organizes FDR procedures along two axes: theoretical versus numerical null distributions, and pooled versus feature-specific null construction. Its value is a framework for checking assumptions and choosing methods, not a new predictive model or a universal performance claim. The assessment here is limited to the preview and figure captions.

Reading scope: Publisher preview and figure captions.

Software update: iq 2.3.0

iq 2.3.0 revises stopping and iteration rules in its existing numerical solver and changes missing-value handling and intensity parsing. These source-level changes warrant attention to numerical convergence and input validation, but they do not establish accuracy or speed gains. maxlfq-bit predates this release.

Reading scope: Source comparison between iq 2.2.0 and 2.3.0; not run or reproduced. Alex A. Henneman is credited as a contributor.
