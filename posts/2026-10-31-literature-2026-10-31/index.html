Literature Brief (October 31, 2026)

Latest2026-10-31 · Beijing
English中文

From rule-based GC–MS classification to adaptive follow-up measurements and differential-interaction prediction, these papers examine how mass-spectrometry evidence informs decisions while keeping simulation, classification and biological inference distinct.

Accessible journal listings and indexes were checked across proteomics, small molecules and lipids, ion mobility, imaging, signal processing and statistics. Access was incomplete for ACS, MCP, some Nature pages and preprint articles. Candidates without verified complete authors or first-public dates were excluded; coverage is not exhaustive.

Automated classification of ignitable liquid profiles: Comparing machine learning and rule-based systems

Leng Leng Chng; Kang Hua Lee

Journal of Forensic Sciences · 2026

First online:
2026-10-21
DOI:
10.1111/1556-4029.70493
Publication status:
Version of Record, Early View

A Python rule framework uses mass spectra and retention times to identify major compounds before classifying GC–MS profiles. Its reported accuracy of 0.922 exceeded 0.635–0.706 for five image-based ML models, supporting domain rules as a serious baseline; the laboratory-generated task does not establish universal superiority or cross-laboratory generalization.

From MS/MS evidence to adaptive spectroscopic characterization: Reinforcement learning guided by candidate molecular structures

Bowen Gao; Lei Zhu; Yiying Wang; Wenjie Yu

arXiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-29
DOI:
10.48550/arXiv.2610.40264
Publication status:
arXiv v1 preprint; not peer reviewed

SI-DQN uses structural disagreement among MS/MS candidates to select complementary spectroscopy and stopping decisions. On 79,462 simulated test samples, positive/negative-start Top-1 accuracy rose from 80.10%/70.44% to 93.78%/93.31% with 0.227/0.340 additional measurements on average; correct-containing candidate sets, equal acquisition costs and unreleased reference code limit immediate laboratory transfer.

Reading scope: Abstract and targeted HTML methods, results, conclusion and code-availability sections; supplements not read, no reproduction.

Predicting Differential Protein-Protein Interactions

Aram Papazian; Volkhard Helms

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-30
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.29.762715
Publication status:
bioRxiv v1 preprint; not peer reviewed

A random forest combines MINT interaction embeddings with condition-specific MS protein abundances to predict interaction changes, using validated PPIs from eight cell lines for training and testing. Separating abundance changes from interaction changes is useful for downstream modeling, but the abstract alone does not establish split independence, class-imbalance handling or numerical performance; these unreviewed predictions are not experimental interaction evidence.

Reading scope: Official bioRxiv indexed abstract and author/date metadata; direct article unavailable, no full-text reading or reproduction.
