Literature Brief (November 24, 2026)

Latest2026-11-24 · Beijing
English中文

Six studies examine how mass spectrometry workflows use retention time, fragmentation, reference libraries and spatial context to improve quantification and interpretation.

More identifications, closer reference matches and lower mass error validate different parts of a workflow. Independent samples and appropriate error checks are still needed to establish transfer to new settings.

MS1-Based Chromatographic Alignment Improves Retention Time Prediction for Multiplexed Targeted Proteomics

Caleb M. Lindgren; David R. Vanderwall; Steven P. Gygi; Steven R. Shuken

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-11-23
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c04781
Publication status:
Version of record

GoDig uses MS1 chromatogram alignment to update target retention times for each run in place of elution-order calibration. The authors report more targets identified and quantified, greater robustness to chromatographic variation, and no need for MS2 scans or real-time searches for that calibration; the abstract gives neither effect sizes nor cross-platform validation.

Casanovo-TMT: deep learning-based de novo sequencing of TMTpro-labeled peptides and phosphopeptides

Han Byeol Kim; Kwang-eun Kim

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-17
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.15.766634
Publication status:
Preprint, v1

Casanovo-TMT extends the residue vocabulary to TMTpro labels and phosphorylation, fine-tunes on about 21 million spectra and reranks candidates by precursor-mass agreement. Reported exact peptidoform agreement was 87.4% and 79.6% on two public global-proteome datasets and 69.2% on a public phosphopeptide-enriched dataset. Reference agreement does not independently establish novel identifications or modification sites, and calibration across datasets cannot be judged from the abstract.

Optimizing Spectral Reference Library Generation Strategies for Data-Independent Acquisition Proteomics in Clinical Cancer Cohorts

Daniel Bucio-Noble; Jennifer M. S. Koh; Erin M. Humphries; Daniela-Lee Smith; Steven G. Williams; Erin K. Sykes; Clare J. Loudon; Dylan Xavier; Natasha Lucas; Phillip J. Robinson; Peter G. Hains

Journal of Proteome Research · 2026

First online:
2026-11-18
DOI:
10.1021/acs.jproteome.5c01025
Publication status:
Version of record

The study compares fractionated DDA, fractionated DIA, a predicted library refined with the same unfractionated sample DIA runs, and gas-phase-fractionated DIA across nine cancer cohorts and 246 specimens. Fractionated DDA gave the most identifications in the prostate dataset, whereas the same-sample refinement strategy had the lowest missingness, still about 40%; more identifications or differential proteins do not establish better quantitative accuracy or the best choice for other cohorts.

Reading scope: Publisher-deposited abstract

msiFlow 2.0: Extending Mass Spectrometry Imaging Workflows with Mass Recalibration, Annotation, Batch Correction, and Visual Quality Control

Philippa Spangenberg; Sebastian Bessler; Eva-Maria Hanschmann; Lars Widera; Jenny Bottek; Stephanie Thiebes; Alexander Potthoff; Olga Shevchuk; Sven Brandau; Jens Soltwisch; Daniel R. Engel

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-13
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.11.765722
Publication status:
Preprint, v1

msiFlow 2.0 adds pixel-wise mass recalibration, lipid annotation, batch correction and visual quality control to an imaging-MS workflow. Across four MALDI-2 or transmission-mode MALDI-2 datasets, the authors report mean absolute mass error below 1.5 ppm for MS/MS-supported lipid features after recalibration and alignment, and an approximately 80% reduction in alignment runtime. The results apply to the tested platforms and feature subset; they are not an overall annotation accuracy estimate, and the abstract does not isolate individual changes.

Deciphering the Biological Function of Tandem Mass Spectra Using a Dual-Modal Metabolite Language Model

Feifan Zhang; Yuting Wang; Yijiang Liu; Jiramet Kinchagawat; Chuchu Wang; Wenya Wang; Yifan Fu; Qingpeng Kong; Xiaotao Shen

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-17
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.16.766993
Publication status:
Preprint, v1

Spec2Function embeds tandem spectra and functional text in a shared space, then retrieves database and literature evidence to generate descriptions without requiring the query spectrum to have a structural assignment. The authors report higher agreement with reference descriptions in metabolite and pathway benchmarks; textual agreement is not direct validation of chemical identity or function, and the abstract does not establish generalization to structures unseen during training.

BioBatchNet: A Dual-Encoder Framework for Robust Batch Effect Correction in Imaging Mass Cytometry

Haiping Liu; Qian Zhao; Shaojie Zhang; Shengzhong Mao; Yuxi Zhou; Yabing Yao; Jingyuan Sun; Andrew Gilmore; Mauricio A Alvarez; Hongpeng Zhou

Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-11-23
DOI:
10.1093/bioinformatics/btag853
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed accepted manuscript

BioBatchNet uses dual encoders and adversarial training to separate batch variation from biological signal, with constrained pairwise clustering to help distinguish overlapping cell populations. The authors compare seven alternatives on three imaging mass cytometry datasets and report a favorable balance between batch mixing and biological preservation. The evidence comes from metal-tagged antibody panels, not label-free MSI; the core method and principal benchmark were already presented in the 2025 preprint.

Reading scope: Official abstract
