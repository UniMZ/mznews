Literature Brief (November 6, 2026)

Latest2026-11-06 · Beijing
English中文

This brief covers prediction-guided fragment indexing for real-time peptide searches and an integrated metabolomics data platform.

Accessible publication metadata, abstracts and publisher-indexed sections were reviewed across computational mass spectrometry. Coverage is partial: some journal and preprint pages were inaccessible, and the reading scope for each paper is stated below.

Improving real-time search through deep learning-enhanced fragment ion indexing

Jimmy Eng; Michael Hoopmann; Devin K. Schweppe; Michael J. MacCoss; William Stafford Noble; Bo Wen

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-03
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.27.762387
Publication status:
Preprint, not peer-reviewed

Comet uses Carafe-predicted fragment intensities to retain only likely observable ions in its search index. Across two search spaces and three index configurations, the abstract reports lower peak memory use, faster searching and comparable peptide-spectrum match counts; prediction is performed before searching and can be amortized across runs using the same database and modifications. Comparable match counts do not establish equal identification accuracy. The effects on weak fragments, error control and generalization remain unverified from the abstract.

Reading scope: Crossref-deposited abstract and bibliographic metadata only; full text and supplementary materials not read, no reproduction.

Metabolomics Subcenter (Met-SC): an integrated metabolomics database and analysis platform

Liliang Wen; Mingchao Wang; Zhongda Zeng; Shuhui Song; Wenming Zhao; Guowang Xu; Xinyu Liu

Nucleic Acids Research · 2026

First online:
2026-11-05
DOI:
10.1093/nar/gkag1127
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article

Met-SC links metabolomics deposition, spectral resources, online analysis and disease-focused knowledge exploration. It reports approximately 7.76 million reference spectra, 73.01 million unknown-feature spectra and 27 analysis and visualization functions. Its main value is traceable data reuse. Evaluation of 30 colorectal-cancer paths is exploratory, not evidence of improved metabolite-identification accuracy; corpus size also does not establish independent chemical coverage or leakage-free training.

Reading scope: Publisher-indexed abstract, publication history, author contributions, resource statistics and selected methods/results sections. Direct article opening was unavailable; supplements and platform functionality were not inspected or reproduced.
