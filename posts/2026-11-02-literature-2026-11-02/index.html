Literature Brief (November 2, 2026)

Latest2026-11-02 · Beijing
English中文

Two approaches examine competing interpretations: challenging non-reference peptide sequences with canonical alternatives, and reranking small-molecule structures through a multi-agent framework. Both compare candidates, while statistical validation must remain distinct from model output.

Accessible journal listings, publication metadata and indexes covered proteomics, small molecules and lipids, ion mobility, imaging and signal processing. Some publisher and preprint articles were inaccessible; the interpretations below retain explicit abstract-level and validation limits.

TransPepMS: A Tool to Verify Peptides Absent from Reference Proteomes

S. Yasin Tabatabaei Dakhili; Olivier Hinse; Leonard J. Foster; Dieter Brömme

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-21
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.20.760571
Publication status:
Preprint, not peer-reviewed; current deposited abstract, revision date not independently verified

TransPepMS challenges non-reference peptide assignments with same-mass canonical alternatives and labels them retained, unresolved or rejected; its abstract reports 53 of 1,154 reported spliced peptides retained, 91 of 99 insulin-spike hybrids retained and 46 planted canonical peptides rejected. The method highlights the distinction between a spectral match and unique sequence evidence, but retention does not prove 53 true splicing events and these challenge sets do not establish global FDR control; details and generalization require full-text assessment.

Reading scope: Current bioRxiv-deposited Crossref abstract, complete authors and first-posted metadata; full text and supplements not read, no reproduction.

MS2Agent: Multi-Agent LLM-Driven Cross-Modal Alignment for Tandem Mass Spectrometry Structural Annotation

Xuemeng Geng; Liu Yang; Hai Jiang

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-31
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c04516
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article, online first

MS2Agent converts spectra and molecular structures into text and uses four agent roles for representation, candidate initialization and collaborative reranking; with the same base language model, CASMI 2022 Recall@1 rises from the Codex baseline of 0.156 to 0.738, suggesting a modular reasoning approach to small-molecule annotation. The abstract also reports MassSpecGym, isomer and real-sample tests, but candidate construction, split independence and possible training-data overlap require full-text scrutiny before extrapolating the benchmark gain to reliable identification of unknown metabolites.

Reading scope: Publisher-deposited abstract and metadata in Crossref; full text and reproduction not assessed
