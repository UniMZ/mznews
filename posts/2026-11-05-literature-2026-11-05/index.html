Literature Brief (November 5, 2026)

Latest2026-11-05 · Beijing
English中文

Molecular neighborhoods, intact masses and retention times provide complementary evidence for distinguishing mass-spectrometry candidates. This brief covers neighborhood-guided metabolite structure inference, large-proteoform identification, the limits of retention-time models, and traceable agent-assisted top-down proteomics.

Accessible journal listings, publication metadata and literature indexes covered proteomics, small molecules and lipids, ion mobility, imaging and signal processing. Some publisher and preprint articles were inaccessible; the interpretations below retain explicit reading and validation limits.

DEEPNET-LLM: Highly accurate structure prediction of unknown metabolites from mass spectra via their molecular neighborhoods

Alexey V Melnik; Andrew Mitchell Pusey Roberts; Alexander Semenov; Dana Moradi; Elizabeth A Coler; Jennifer Goldstein; Bibhusha Ojha; Nathan J Swift; Suada Demirovic; Mark W Peczuh; Thomas D Horvath; Melinda A Engevik; Vladimir Boginski; Alexander A Aksenov

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-03
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.28.762558
Publication status:
Preprint, not peer-reviewed

DEEPNET-LLM narrows a query spectrum to a local chemical neighborhood using Molecular Community Networking, then uses LLM reasoning to transfer structural information from neighbors. The abstract reports 84.7% top-1 accuracy across 2,249 spectra of 900 previously unpublished standards and 85.9% on a random MassSpecGym subset excluding stereochemistry. Neighborhood-guided generation is promising, but private spectra alone do not exclude structure-level or neighbor-related leakage; the splits, availability of useful neighbors and independent generalization require full-text evaluation.

An Integrated Experimental and Informatics Strategy Enables Large Proteoform Identification in Top-Down Proteomics

Matthew S Fischer; Eli J Larson; Ryan K Schroeder; Hsin-Ju Chan; Heewon Choi; Thomas S Weir; Andrew J Perciaccante; Carmen C Sucharov; Yang Lu; Ying Ge

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-02
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.30.763218
Publication status:
Preprint, not peer-reviewed

This workflow combines large-protein separation and acquisition with precursor mass correction, intact-mass-guided candidate discovery and MS/MS validation, enabling conventional searches of DDA data with unresolved precursor isotope envelopes and enhancing fragment signals with DIA. The abstract reports 703 nonredundant proteoforms from 236 proteins in human heart tissue, including 65 proteins above 30 kDa and 22 above 50 kDa, with identifications extending to 223 kDa. The integration is useful for method development, but these are whole-workflow results rather than an isolated algorithmic gain; error control and generalization require full-text evaluation.

Structure-based retention time prediction for fentanyl analogs under cyano and phenyl-hexyl SCC-LC–MS/MS conditions: supporting discrimination among MS-ambiguous candidates

Hwa-Yong Jang; Eunwoo Choi; Sunghee Choi; Nakyung Kim; Jiyu Kim; Sumin Seo; Eunyoung Han; Sang Beom Han; Han Bin Oh

Journal of Analytical Science and Technology · 2026

First online:
2026-11-02
DOI:
10.1186/s40543-026-00578-5
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article

Using 94 structure–retention-time records, the study compares five regressors with nested cross-validation, structure-grouped checks and applicability-domain analysis for prioritizing MS-ambiguous candidates. The selected Gaussian-process model has a leave-one-out MAE of 1.15 minutes; RT ordering is correct for 86% of more widely separated candidate pairs versus 68% of close pairs, while rare local modifications can cause large errors even inside the applicability domain. These are internal results under one serial-column method, not external validation or confirmatory identification; the local-support and candidate-spacing analyses are the transferable contribution.

TDEaseNexus: A Traceable, Open-Source AI Agent Platform for Top-Down Proteomics Data Analysis

Rui Qian; Meng-Ting Zhang; Yucheng Liao; Tao Yi; Kai Lin; Xingpu Zhao; Jian-Zhang Pan; Weijie Zhang; Qun Fang; Mowei Zhou

ChemRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-03
DOI:
10.26434/chemrxiv.15011633/v1
Publication status:
Preprint v1, not peer-reviewed

TDEaseNexus uses a natural-language agent to coordinate top-down proteomics analysis, combining workflow execution, proteoform interpretation and interactive visualization in a desktop platform. Domain-specific skills and Rust tools support modification searches, mass calibration and calculations of spectral evidence while recording analysis provenance. The platform may reduce friction between tools and make computational steps inspectable, but the abstract provides neither independent performance benchmarks nor error-rate validation, so it does not establish improved identification accuracy.
