Literature digest · October 11, 2026

Latest2026-10-11 · Beijing
English中文

Seven recent papers cover isotope prediction, structural modeling, formula and polymer-composition analysis, biologics data interpretation and acquisition integration.

The central question is how to incorporate experimental constraints into testable models while distinguishing simulations, author-reported results and independent validation. Coverage is incomplete because some ACS, MCP and other journal sources were inaccessible.

Evaluating a Lightweight Neural Network for Aggregate Isotope Distribution Prediction

John G. Pavek; Josiah Grimes; Brian L. Frey; Nathan V. Welham; Lloyd M. Smith; Michael T. Marty

Journal of Proteome Research · 2026

First online:
2026-10-09
DOI:
10.1021/acs.jproteome.6c00685
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article.

IsoGen adds lightweight neural-network prediction of aggregate isotope envelopes alongside Fourier-transform calculation and supports retraining for perturbed isotope ratios. The authors report comparable prediction under natural ratios and demonstrate training from experimental envelopes, making adaptation to poorly specified isotope compositions the main attraction; this is neither a universal replacement for FFT nor fine-isotope-structure prediction.

Reading scope: Publisher abstract/metadata and official repository documentation; full paper and implementation not audited.

Methods for inferring interaction potentials from cross-linking mass spectrometry data

Börries von Seggern; Mohsen Sadeghi

The Journal of Chemical Physics · 2026

First online:
2026-10-09
DOI:
10.1063/5.0346848
Publication status:
Journal version; an earlier preprint appeared on June 4, 2026. October 9 is the online date; October 14 is the issue date.

This framework connects cross-link contact observables with inverse statistical mechanics and compares gradient and predictor–corrector algorithms for fitting coarse-grained interactions in phase-separating mixtures. Ten-component and three-phase models demonstrate parameter recovery, but validation uses simulated reference systems and synthetic XL-MS-like observations, not new measured XL-MS experiments or unique inversion of coordination numbers.

Reading scope: Selected publisher-indexed theory, computational details and conclusions; no code audit or independent reproduction.

Integrating Ion Mobility Mass Spectrometry Data with AlphaFold and Rosetta Improves Protein Complex Structure Prediction

Akshaya Narayanasamy; Zachary C. Drake; S. M. Bargeen A. Turzo; Amber D. Rolland; James S. Prell; Vicki H. Wysocki; Steffen Lindert

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-08
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c01893
Publication status:
Journal version. Results quoted here use the journal’s normalized RMSD100 and 14 threshold-crossing cases, not the earlier preprint figures.

AlphaFold-predicted subunits are docked with Rosetta, then selected using measured or simulated collision cross sections. The journal abstract reports better normalized RMSD100 for 26 of 38 complexes than AlphaFold-Multimer, including 14 moving from above 4 Å to below 4 Å; global-shape constraints are promising for model selection, but improvement is not universal and was not independently reproduced here.

Reading scope: Journal abstract, author metadata, publication history and supplementary inventory; full text and supplementary data not read.

Improving Molecular Formula Assignment of Nitrogen- and Sulfur-Rich DOM in Ultrahigh-Resolution Mass Spectrometry: Validation of Oligopeptides

Peter Herzsprung; Oliver J. Lechtenfeld; Wolf von Tümpling; Norbert Kamjunke; Shuxian Gao

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-10
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c04588
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article.

Peptide elemental composition and amino-acid combinations constrain ambiguous molecular formulas in nitrogen- and sulfur-rich dissolved organic matter. The abstract reports stoichiometric support for about 36% and 30% of nitrogen-rich formulas in two near-isobaric substitution families; these interpretable constraints help assess formula plausibility but do not establish peptide sequence or unique structure.

Reading scope: Official journal listing and publisher-deposited abstract/metadata; full paper not read.

BioDRUMs: An Open-Source Python Pipeline for Automated Mean Drug-to-Antibody Ratio and Biologics Structural Integrity Analysis from High-Resolution Mass Spectrometry Data

Andrea Di Ianni; Francesco Molinaro; Luca M. Barbero; Kyra Cowan; Federico Riccardi Sirtori

Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-05
DOI:
10.1021/jasms.6c00289
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article.

BioDRUMs performs vendor-neutral postprocessing of deconvoluted intact-mass tables, generating degradation hypotheses, mean drug-to-antibody ratios and structural-integrity summaries through Python and graphical interfaces. Reported use cases include in-vitro stability and in-vivo samples; it flags isobaric candidate ambiguity rather than separating individual contributions, and neither general software superiority nor drug efficacy follows from these results.

Reading scope: Selected original publisher-indexed Methods, Results and Conclusions; no complete manuscript download or independent reproduction.

Lost in Overlap: Mapping Local Composition Landscapes to Predict the Upper Measurable Mass Limit of Copolymers and Terpolymers by MALDI-TOF MS

Gergő Róth; Ákos Kuki; Tibor Nagy; Sándor Kéki

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-08
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c05591
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article.

The algorithm enumerates feasible local copolymer and terpolymer compositions and combines monomer-incorporation statistics, isotope distributions and measured peak widths to predict overlap and an upper mass limit for compositional discrimination. It connects chemical ambiguity with instrumental resolution, but numerical limits depend on the instrument and discrimination criterion rather than being universal analyte constants.

Reading scope: Official ACS listing and publisher-deposited abstract/metadata; full text, code and numerical benchmarks not audited.

TherMIDAS: An Integrated Data Acquisition System for FT-ICR Mass Spectrometry

Jesse D. Canterbury; Michael W. Senko; Lydia Babcock-Adams; Marek Polák; Christopher L. Hendrickson; Kristina Håkansson; Chad R. Weisbrod

Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-08
DOI:
10.1021/jasms.6c00273
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article.

TherMIDAS integrates newer PXIe control boards with vendor software to couple an Orbitrap Eclipse front end to 14.5 T and 21 T FT-ICR instruments. The authors report duty-cycle gains from parallel analyzers, making it useful for instrument-control and data-processing integration; the abstract provides no comparable numerical gain and does not establish general performance superiority.

Reading scope: Official ACS contents and publisher-deposited abstract/metadata; full text and implementation details not reviewed.
