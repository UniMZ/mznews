October 20, 2026 Literature Brief

Latest2026-10-20 · Beijing
English中文

Three papers explore reproducible data reuse and analysis: agent-supported reanalysis, spectral filters for single-cell clustering, and an integrated R metabolomics workflow.

The most useful next step is to examine intervention logging and graph-filter ablations. Coverage is limited to accessible publisher and indexed records; some online-first feeds and full texts were unavailable.

Omics data discovery agents: Agent-supported retrieval, reanalysis, and synthesis of published omics data

Alexandre Hutton; Jesse G. Meyer

PLOS Computational Biology · 2026

First online:
2026-10-06
DOI:
10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014822
Publication status:
Journal article; earlier preprint available

ODDA connects parameter extraction, data retrieval and containerized reanalysis with provenance and intervention records, offering a useful design for auditable workflows. The authors completed five reanalyses, including one DDA and two DIA studies, but every case needed human guidance or workflow accommodations; these demonstrations do not establish unattended success rates, and corpus-scale extraction lacks an independent accuracy benchmark.

Reading scope: Publisher main text; no independent reproduction

scProfiterole: Clustering of Single-Cell Proteomic Data Using Graph Contrastive Learning via Spectral Filters

Mustafa Coşkun; Filipa Blasco Lopes; Pınar Kubilay Tolunay; Mark R. Chance; Mehmet Koyutürk

Journal of Computational Biology · 2026

First online:
2026-10-09
DOI:
10.1177/15578666261491608
Publication status:
Online-first journal article; earlier preprint available

scProfiterole stabilizes polynomial interpolation of spectral graph filters through Arnoldi orthonormalization and learns filter coefficients for contrastive clustering of single-cell proteomics. The earlier preprint compares filters and clustering baselines on SCoPE2 and combined N2/nanoPOTS data, making its initialization and ablation design useful; limited cell types and dependence on upstream graph construction constrain generalization, and better clustering does not establish missing-value recovery.

Reading scope: Journal abstract and selected earlier-preprint Methods/Results; journal full text and supplements not verified

mums2: an R package for database-dependent and -independent analysis of untargeted metabolomics data

Gregory Johnson, Jr.; Allison R. Mason; Joseph M. Krampen; Jennifer N. T. Nguyen; Marcy J. Balunas; Patrick D. Schloss

Microbiology Resource Announcements · 2026

First online:
2026-10-14
DOI:
10.1128/mra.00780-26
Publication status:
Journal software announcement

mums2 integrates feature QC, library matching, formula prediction and spectral clustering in a local R workflow, organizing features into operational metabolomic units (OMUs) for ecological analysis. The authors report 7.6 minutes and 6 GB RAM for 267 samples on 14 threads; this is a runtime example, not a comprehensive benchmark, and OMUs are not confirmed compounds. Its main value lies in modular integration and interoperable data structures.

Reading scope: Publisher main text and project documentation; software not run
