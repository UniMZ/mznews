Literature Brief (November 15, 2026)

Latest2026-11-15 · Beijing
English中文

Ion mobility can improve mass-spectrometry selectivity, but additional peaks do not necessarily represent additional structural isomers. Today's selections examine that distinction and a software correction that can change previously processed profiling data.

The evidence spans a metabolomics preprint and a software release, rather than independent replication. Full-text evaluation and computational reproduction were not performed.

Using XCiMS to Quantify the Benefits of Ion Mobility Spectrometry for Untargeted Metabolomics

Lingjue Wang; Kevin Cho; Junjie Yang; Ethan Stancliffe; Michaela Schwaiger-Haber; Clark S Hofstrand; Triston Groff; James N Dodds; Leah P Shriver; Dhanalakshmi S Anbukumar; Erin S Baker; Gary J Patti

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-26
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.21.760801
Publication status:
Preprint

XCiMS detects peaks after collapsing LC–IMS–MS data into pseudo-3D space, then extracts 4D signals from 3D-guided regions of interest. In human plasma, up to half of 3D peaks yielded multiple 4D peaks with high-resolution IMS, largely charge isomers and ion clusters rather than newly resolved structural isomers; with a 16-minute separation, chimeric MS/MS frequency fell by about half. Findings depend on samples and acquisition settings.

Reading scope: Author abstract indexed in PubMed and verified bioRxiv metadata; full text not read and results not reproduced.

Tanmay Tanna

Publication status:
Software revision: winn 0.1.6

winn 0.1.6 fixes a formula error that silently prevented the intended spline-based drift correction, causing fallback models instead. The release restores documented smoothers and adds regression tests; authors say results change for every drift-corrected feature, making earlier analyses worth checking. This correctness update has no independent quantitative validation of downstream accuracy.

Reading scope: Official metadata, release notes and README; software not run and results not reproduced.
