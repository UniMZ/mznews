Literature digest · October 15, 2026

Latest2026-10-15 · Beijing
English中文

Today’s papers address reusable training data for proteomics and integrated analysis of mass cytometry.

MassNet is the priority for deeper reading, especially the distinction between scaling gains and cross-domain generalization. Several journal sources were inaccessible, so coverage is incomplete.

MassNet: billion-scale AI-ready mass spectral corpus empowering scalable deep learning in proteomics

Jun A.; Xiang Zhang; Xiaofan Zhang; Jiaqi Wei; Te Zhang; Yamin Deng; Pu Liu; Zongxiang Nie; Yiwen Yu; Yi Chen; Nanqing Dong; Zhiqiang Gao; Siqi Sun; Tiannan Guo

Nature Methods · 2026

First online:
2026-10-14
DOI:
10.1038/s41592-026-03247-y
Publication status:
Version of record; earlier bioRxiv preprint in 2025

MassNet standardizes 1.54 billion DDA spectra across 35 species in the traceable MSDT format and supports XuanjiNovo, a nonautoregressive sequencing model trained with progressively masked peptide labels. On peptide-disjoint validation, scaling training from 30 million to 100 million PSMs raised peptide recall from about 0.54 to 0.68. Cross-platform gains are uneven: the 100-million model trails competitors on most nine-species benchmark datasets until additional fine-tuning, making domain-shift evaluation as important as data scale.

Reading scope: Reviewed indexed publisher main-text, methods, benchmark and author sections; supplementary tables, raw data and code were not audited or reproduced.

Seumetry: a versatile and comprehensive R toolkit to accelerate high-dimensional flow and mass cytometry data analysis

Malte Borggrewe; Markus Flosbach; Lucia Testa; Cedric Ly; Martin Baumdick; Immo Prinz; Hamburg Intestinal Tissue Study Group; Elena Billeb; Stefan Bonn; Madeleine J Bunders

Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-10-13
DOI:
10.1093/bioinformatics/btag730
Publication status:
Accepted manuscript; earlier 2024 bioRxiv preprint

Seumetry combines flow- and mass-cytometry processing with Seurat for quality control, visualization, population-abundance testing and protein-expression analysis. The authors report agreement with manual gating on public datasets alongside an intestinal immune-cell example. Its value is an integrated workflow for single-cell marker intensities; the abstract does not establish numerical gains, rare-population error rates or superiority over other toolkits.

Reading scope: Publisher abstract, full byline including consortium, date and accepted-manuscript status reviewed; PDF, supplementary benchmarks and code not reviewed or reproduced.
