Literature Brief (October 28, 2026)

Latest2026-10-28 · Beijing
English中文

Two studies connect measurements acquired under different conditions: retention-time transfer links chromatographic feature tables, while paired EAD/CID spectra help evaluate cross-fragmentation library searches. Both emphasize coverage, ambiguity and the limits of annotation.

Coverage spans accessible primary metadata and scientific excerpts across proteomics, small molecules, lipids, ion mobility and imaging. Some publisher pages and feeds were unavailable; this is not an exhaustive survey. Reading scope is stated for each paper; no results were reproduced.

Retention-Time Bridging across Gradients for LC-HRMS Feature Correspondence

Wentao Zhu; Elle Harwood; Daniel Raftery

Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-22
DOI:
10.1021/jasms.6c00226
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed article

RTBridge selects mass-unique, cross-method mass-consistent and abundance-concordant anchors to link short- and long-gradient LC-HRMS feature tables without molecular structures, comparing six retention-time transfer models. PCHIP recovered 5,595 primary feature pairs across four matrix/mode datasets with a mean median RT error of 0.0232 minutes; residual-based hard filtering slightly improved error but reduced coverage, highlighting an accuracy–recovery tradeoff. This establishes feature correspondence, not metabolite identification; transfer across platforms, columns and laboratories remains unvalidated.

From Single Injection to Dual Insights: Integrating EAD and CID Acquisition for Spectral Library Generation and EAD–CID Spectral Matching

Lisa Panzenboeck; Christina Brenner; Veronika Fitz; Mingxun Wang; Patrick Mueller; Evelyn Rampler; Yasin El Abiead; Gunda Koellensperger

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-02
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c01204
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed article

A single-injection, multi-energy EAD/CID workflow generated a resource of 459 CID and 414 EAD spectra from 45 steroid standards, supporting spectral-library expansion and fragmentation-model development. EAD spectra could also match existing CID/HCD libraries, but isomer mismatches remained prominent: public-data hits are putative annotations, not substitutes for orthogonal confirmation such as reference-standard retention times. The cross-fragmentation matching evidence is useful, although generalization beyond steroids remains untested.
