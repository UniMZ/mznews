Literature Brief (November 22, 2026)

Latest2026-11-22 · Beijing
English中文

Four studies illustrate how computational methods use structure, reference data and spatial context in mass spectrometry. Each raises a practical question: what does the comparison actually validate?

Across the studies, composition agreement does not establish topology, a cross-platform reference is not ground truth, and performance on held-out regions is not evidence of generalization to new patients.

Glycoverse: a multi-functional R framework for glycomics and glycoproteomics data analysis

Bin Fu; Xisong Feng; Chenxin Li; Chenyang Zhao; Jiawei Fan; Ying Zhang; Haojie Lu

Nature Communications · 2026

First online:
2026-11-20
DOI:
10.1038/s41467-026-79409-5
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed accepted article

Glycoverse provides interoperable R tools for analyzing glycan structures and motifs across glycans, sites and proteins. In a mouse tissue reanalysis, the authors found tissue-specific branching and terminal motifs; the four search outputs were matched at the composition level, while the topology came from StrucGP. This is a downstream interpretation framework, not an independent validation of glycan structures or a new search engine.

Reading scope: Accepted manuscript: selected results and methods; companion analysis script

DreamAI2: an imputation tool for data-independent acquisition proteomics data

Jessica Ren; Vladislav Petyuk; Tao Liu; Pei Wang; Weiping Ma

Bioinformatics Advances · 2026

First online:
2026-11-06
DOI:
10.1093/bioadv/vbag312
Publication status:
Journal article

DreamAI2 benchmarks missing-value imputation for label-free DIA against paired TMT-DDA measurements from the same tumors, rather than relying only on masking observed values. Its fast ensemble omits MissForest while retaining similar performance in this benchmark. The reference is a cross-platform proxy restricted to selected concordant proteins from one cohort, not ground truth or evidence of peptide-level transfer.

Reading scope: Selected methods, results and conclusion

EncNovo: De Novo Peptide Sequencing With an Encoder‐Only Architectures

Xinglong Zhu; Zejiang Chen; Zihao Huang; Haipeng Wang

Rapid Communications in Mass Spectrometry · 2026

First online:
2026-11-16
DOI:
10.1002/rcm.70211
Publication status:
Journal article

EncNovo uses a non-autoregressive encoder to process precursor, spectrum and sequence-prediction tokens together, with attention informed by peak mass differences. The authors report higher peptide recall than Casanovo and RefineNovo at full prediction coverage on a nine-species benchmark. The abstract does not establish that the gain persists at a fixed error rate or transfers to unfamiliar instruments or modified peptides.

Reading scope: Publisher abstract and data availability statement

Classification of Tissues on MALDI-MSI Image Modality for Pancreatic Cancer Studies

Abubakar Qahir; Jenny Benois-Pineau; Victoria Bourgeais; Fabien Baldacci; Sandrine Dabernat; Nicolas Desbenoit; Mélanie Moreau; Mehdi Boubaddi; Marion Marty

arXiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-17
DOI:
10.48550/arXiv.2611.22680
Publication status:
Preprint, v2 (November 19)

This prototype converts MALDI-MSI data into ion images and uses entropy to select images and patches for a compact Swin classifier. High accuracy on held-out regions of pancreatic sections shows the workflow can discriminate tissue regions within its case study, but models were fitted separately by patient and polarity. The study does not establish cross-patient generalization, and the separate Barrett’s esophagus comparison showed no significant advantage over the alternatives tested.

Reading scope: Preprint v2 full text
