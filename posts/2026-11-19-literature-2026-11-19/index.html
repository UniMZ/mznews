Today's papers examine channel spillover correction, learning from unlabeled tandem mass spectra and stable feature selection. Each entry states its reading scope; no results were independently reproduced.

A zero-shot spillover denoiser for highly multiplexed images

Jason Lequyer; Ferris Nowlan; Wen-Hsin Hsu; Reuben Philip; Anna Christina Erpf; Angela M. Y. Chan; Christine Tran; Ben X. Wang; Zoltan Maliga; Hartland Warren Jackson; Laurence Pelletier

Nature Methods · 2026

First online:
2026-11-18
DOI:
10.1038/s41592-026-03263-y
Publication status:
Version of record

SpillOT trains a denoiser on the multiplexed tissue image itself, then uses optimal transport to compare local patches and suppress channel spillover, without an external training set or a premeasured compensation matrix. In the authors’ imaging mass cytometry (IMC) example, correlations between biologically mutually exclusive markers fell after correction; the method was also evaluated on multiplex immunofluorescence data. It assumes uniform noise, and reduced marker correlation alone does not establish that genuine co-expression was preserved.

Reading scope: Abstract and selected methods and results read; not reproduced

SemiNovo: Learning Beyond Search-Identified Spectra for De Novo Peptide Sequencing

Pan Liu; Dongxin Lyu; Yusen Tan; Yifan Li; Hai-tao Yu; Yunhua Zhong; Zikun Wang; Tianze Ling; Jun Xia

arXiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-16
Publication status:
Preprint v1

SemiNovo brings 4.5 million quality-filtered, unlabeled tandem mass spectra into semi-supervised de novo peptide sequencing, using mass constraints and path confidence to select reliable sequence prefixes as pseudo-labels. In the authors’ update-matched Nine-species NovoBench comparison, semi-supervision raised exact peptide matching from 0.587 to 0.635 across all test spectra; on Seven-species, however, PTM precision fell from 0.495 to 0.382 versus the supervised checkpoint, and precision–coverage AUC also declined slightly. The results support testing unlabeled spectra for full-peptide recovery, but spectrum-level deduplication does not establish peptide-level independence.

Reading scope: Methods, results and appendix read; not reproduced

A Stable Feature Screening Framework Integrating Autoencoder and SMOTE for Imbalanced Metabolomics

Qing Ye; Zeng Deng; Xin Xie; Qiang Huang; Jigen Luo

Biology · 2026

First online:
2026-11-14
DOI:
10.3390/biology15222089
Publication status:
Version of record

AE-SMOTE combines dimensionality reduction, denoising and minority-class oversampling for high-dimensional imbalanced metabolomics, asking whether different training splits select similar features. The authors compared eight simulated and three real metabolomics datasets; on ST000385, the Kuncheva stability index was 0.771 with 40 selected features. This provides a comparison point for feature-selection workflows, but stability within cross-validation is not biomarker replication in an independent cohort.

Reading scope: Abstract and selected methods and results read; not reproduced
