Two studies highlight how explicit calibration assumptions and traceable structural evidence can improve mass-spectrometry analysis.

Correcting topographic artifacts in three-dimensional ToF-SIMS: A practical guide from data-only reconstruction to correlative AFM

Jacob Shusterman; Anton V. Ievlev

arXiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-20
DOI:
10.48550/arXiv.2610.28593
Publication status:
Preprint, arXiv v1

This comparison tests SIMS-only, endpoint-AFM and multistage-AFM topographic corrections on a device with known geometry. Data-only depth calibration depends on layer-thickness assumptions; endpoint AFM misses changing local sputter rates, while multistage imaging improves reconstruction at extra acquisition cost. These calibration boundaries are useful for reconstruction software, but one device does not establish broad sample validation.

A Transformation-Guided Mass Spectrometry Workflow for Expanding In Vivo Metabolite Coverage and Revealing Noncanonical Metabolism of Epigallocatechin Gallate

Zongde Jiang; Shengmin Sang

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-21
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c02274
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed article

The workflow combines iterative acquisition, transformation rules, FISh fragment prediction and multistage MS evidence for metabolite annotation. Its EGCG case yielded 702 candidates, including 343 tiered annotations and 359 structurally unresolved signals; the reusable lesson is traceable evidence, not equating candidates with confirmed identifications. Generalization beyond one parent compound and manual review remains unestablished.

Reading scope: Publisher-indexed abstract and selected methods passages; no complete full-text/supplement review or reproduction.
