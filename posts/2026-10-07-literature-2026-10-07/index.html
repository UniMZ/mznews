Literature digest · October 7, 2026

Latest2026-10-07 · Beijing
English中文

Today we introduce four recent papers on computational methods for mass spectrometry: three journal articles and one preprint. The listed versions appeared online between September 29 and October 5, 2026; 2-step and PiProteline had earlier preprints.

The papers cover retention-time prediction, self-supervised spectrum learning, targeted quantification and proteomics analysis, with attention to transfer conditions, evaluation baselines and quality control.

This digest is a selection with limited coverage of recent ACS, Molecular & Cellular Proteomics and Talanta articles. Reading scope is stated for each paper. No independent experimental or computational reproduction was performed.

Times are changing but order matters: transferable prediction of small-molecule liquid chromatography retention times

Fleming Kretschmer; Eva-Maria Harrieder; Michael Witting; Sebastian Böcker

Nature Methods · 2026

First online:
2026-10-01
DOI:
10.1038/s41592-026-03243-2
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article. An earlier ChemRxiv preprint exists; the first-online date shown is for the journal version, not the first disclosure of the study.

2-step predicts retention order with a molecular graph model, then maps it to retention times using high-confidence anchors from the target reversed-phase LC system. In chemically heterogeneous splits, the authors report better performance on five of six datasets and a near tie on the sixth. Even without target-system training data, transfer needs target-system anchors, column descriptors and pH. HILIC is outside the demonstrated scope; some biological examples lack authentic-standard confirmation, and the evaluation splits may remain optimistic.

Reading scope: Main text and selected Methods checked. Supplementary materials, raw data and code were not fully audited; no independent reproduction.

dIon: Fragmentation-Based Invariance for Self-Supervised Learning of Tandem Mass Spectra

Alfred Nilsson; Joel Lapin; Samuel H. Payne; Mathias Wilhelm; Lukas Käll

arXiv, cs.LG · 2026

First online:
2026-10-05
DOI:
10.48550/arXiv.2610.06282
Publication status:
arXiv v1 preprint, not peer reviewed. Submitted 2026-10-05 at 13:09:47 UTC. arXiv lists the DOI with DataCite registration pending; use the arXiv paper link.

dIon learns from precursor-conditioned spectrum mixtures and precursor-withheld partial spectra to emphasize fragment evidence. Under matched downstream training, the authors report a 2.3–8.4 percentage-point gain in peptide precision over training from scratch. Frozen representations do not beat a simple binned spectral-angle baseline; the strongest retrieval needs supervised adaptation. Search-engine-derived labels may carry selection bias and identification errors, and computation is costly. This preprint evaluates peptides/proteomics; transfer to metabolomics is unproven.

Reading scope: Preprint main text, Tables 1–3 and selected appendices checked. Remaining appendices and code were not fully audited; no independent reproduction.

MRMhub: a scalable data-processing framework for large-scale targeted metabolomics

Bo Burla; Guo Shou Teo; Peter Imre Benke; Zinan Lu; Sock Hwee Tan; Shanshan Ji; Jeongah Oh; Pei Yen Lim; Vaitheeswari; Liang Gao; Amaury Cazenave-Gassiot; Kavita Venkataraman; E Shyong Tai; Federico Torta; Markus Rene Wenk; Mark Yan Yee Chan; Hyungwon Choi

Nature Metabolism · 2026

First online:
2026-09-29
DOI:
10.1038/s42255-026-01629-2
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal publication, Correspondence.

MRMhub integrates targeted MRM peak processing, quantification and QC. INTEGRATOR uses cross-sample chromatographic patterns to address retention-time drift and interference; QUANT handles validation, quantification, artefact correction, filtering and QC reports. Official examples cover 937 samples with 503 features and 4,591 samples with 829 features, demonstrating scale rather than independently validated accuracy. Without the complete paper and supplementary benchmarks, accuracy advantages or speedups cannot be assessed here. Its results do not establish untargeted metabolite identification performance.

Reading scope: Publisher preview/indexed text, author metadata and official documentation checked. The complete paper and supplementary benchmarks were not obtained; no independent reproduction.

PiProteline: An R Package for Integrated Proteomics Data Analysis, from Label-Free Quantitation to Systems Biology

Andrea Lomagno; Saleh Hamed; Ishak Yusuf; Pietro Luigi Mauri; Dario Di Silvestre

Biology · 2026

First online:
2026-10-01
DOI:
10.3390/biology15191723
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed journal article. An earlier preprint was posted on 2026-07-14 (10.20944/preprints202607.0993.v1); the first-online date shown is for the journal version.

PiProteline integrates label-free abundance preprocessing, differential analysis, enrichment and protein-interaction networks in R/Shiny. On a spike-in benchmark, the authors report performance similar to DEP and protti, below limma. Per-sample-maximum merging of same-gene isoforms loses isoform information. Weighted networks call for 20–30 samples per group; pairwise-complete correlations do not resolve missing-data mechanisms. The empirical composite network measure is not a calibrated discovery probability. Input must be abundance-scale; log2 intensities are not automatically back-transformed.

Reading scope: Available publisher text, including Methods, Results and Discussion, and the official repository checked. The PDF and supplements were not reviewed in full; supplementary Figure S1 was not independently rerun.
