October 27, 2026 Literature Brief

Latest2026-10-27 · Beijing
English中文

Three studies address complementary computational challenges: reviewing glycopeptide identifications, interpreting ambiguous spatial metabolite assignments, and compressing large imaging datasets without losing regional information.

Coverage spans accessible primary metadata and abstracts across proteomics, small molecules, lipids, ion mobility and imaging. Some publisher pages and feeds were unavailable; this is not an exhaustive survey. All three assessments are abstract-based, and two papers are preprints.

Benchmarking Glycoproteomics Software Using Diagnostic Ion Based Post-Validation for Glycopeptide Identification

Hiroaki Sakaue; Kunio Kawanishi; Azusa Tomioka; Chiaki Nagai-Okatani; Hiroyuki Kaji; Atsushi Kuno

Journal of Proteome Research · 2026

First online:
2026-10-01
DOI:
10.1021/acs.jproteome.6c00345
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed article

A comparison of five glycopeptide search tools on BJAB-K20 cell data found that approximately one-third of identifications were unique to individual tools. Post-validation using diagnostic oxonium and Y0/Y1 or b/y ions offers a transferable way to review complementary search results. The study focuses on rare NeuGc/KDN glycans; spectral-evidence tiers are not calibrated error-rate estimates, and broader transferability requires validation. Abstract only.

SpacePath enables pathway and molecular-family analysis beyond annotation ambiguity in spatial metabolomics and lipidomics

Johanna Galvis; Mélanie Moreau; Sebastian Lillo; Doriane Bomont; Léa Ledoux; Yanis Zirem; Michel Salzet; Isabelle Fournier; Nicolas Desbenoit; Thomas Daubon; Macha Nikolski

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-22
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.16.759546
Publication status:
Preprint v1; not peer reviewed

SpacePath preserves ion-to-compound ambiguity while combining spatial pathway scoring with chemically similar molecular families; four public spatial-lipidomics datasets recovered known patterns, and MS/MS supported four tumor-associated features in new glioblastoma data. Abstract only: the reported increase from about 1% to 24% concerns assignment coverage, not identification accuracy; the useful design is broader spatial interpretation without hiding annotation uncertainty.

CPMsi: a data-driven Computational Pipeline for fast, large-scale Mass Spectrometry Imaging analysis

Paola Tasca; Iulia Sidorov; Hans J. Dalebout; Nina Ogrinc; Ron M. A. Heeren; Aiko P. de Vries; Jesper Kers; Manfred Wuhrer; Ton J. Rabelink; Martin Giera; Yassene Mohammed

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-21
DOI:
10.64898/2026.10.15.758756
Publication status:
Preprint v1; not peer reviewed

CPMsi combines global spectral binning, molecular-region detection and consensus spectra for large MSI datasets and cross-section or multimodal integration; the authors report approximately 30-fold faster region detection than vendor software on brain sections with roughly 500,000–600,000 pixels. Abstract only: this stage-specific benchmark does not establish an end-to-end or clinical-cohort speedup, but the region-preserving compression and cross-section mapping are useful computational designs.

Reading scope: Original abstract and bibliographic metadata only; no full-text review or reproduction
