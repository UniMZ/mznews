Literature Brief (November 23, 2026)

Latest2026-11-23 · Beijing
English中文

Four studies examine how mass spectrometry workflows use spectral references, database-search evidence and quality controls to turn signals into interpretable results.

Reference coverage, identification counts and QC metrics answer different questions. Authentic standards, comparable error-rate estimates and external datasets remain important checks. Some journal and preprint pages were unavailable; those entries are based on the reading scope shown.

Objective Evaluation of Untargeted Metabolomic Methods Based on Data-Dependent Acquisition and Public Spectral Databases

Romina Pacheco-Tapia; Inés Castro-Dionicio; Mickaël Chevalier; Coralie Berthier; Jean-Michel Lecerf; Amélie Bonnefond; Philippe Froguel; Francesc Puig-Castellví; Marc-Emmanuel Dumas

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-11-19
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c03292
Publication status:
Version of record

The authors compare 23 DDA LC–MS workflows across six platforms, using public GNPS library annotations and ranking workflows by reproducibility, dilution linearity and chemical-class diversity. Across all workflows they report 2,448 putative MSI level 2 annotations and confirm all 129 compounds for which their lab had authentic standards; the latter is a selected subset, not an error-rate estimate for all annotations, and apparent coverage still depends on the library.

MSGFLEX: An optimized end-to-end pipeline for proteomics and metaproteomics analysis built on MS-GF+ with a dedicated rescoring model XPECTRA

Tulasi Rao Relangi; Harrison A Hall; Aaron T Wright

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-20
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.19.767922
Publication status:
Preprint, v1

MSGFLEX couples MS-GF+ searches with XPECTRA, a cross-validated XGBoost ensemble that combines its predictions with search evidence to rerank peptide–spectrum matches, and adds peptide-to-protein quantification. The authors report more peptide and protein identifications than three rescoring alternatives across four benchmark datasets, but the abstract does not establish comparable realized error rates or generalization to independent cohorts.

High-throughput electrochemical transformation expands mass spectrometric reference space for transformation products

Erik Niehaves; Manuel Gross; Giovanni Andrea Vitale; Steffen Heuckeroth; Thilo Hofmann; Uwe Karst; Daniel Petras; Valentin Goeldner

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-19
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.14.765618
Publication status:
Preprint, v1

Online electrochemistry and tandem MS generated a library of 1,341 suspect transformation-product entries from 112 starting chemicals. Searches of public untargeted data matched 214 electrochemical transformation products; the authors report that 70% of those matches lacked an annotation from conventional open spectral libraries. These matches are leads for follow-up, not confirmation of structure, sample provenance or a biological transformation pathway.

Reading scope: Official abstract

MetaboRx: R Shiny application for quality assessment and preprocessing of mass spectrometry-based metabolomics data

Breanna K. Guppy; Ronald A. Merrill; Reid F. Brown; T. Idil Apak Evans; Eric B. Taylor

bioRxiv · 2026

First online:
2026-11-18
DOI:
10.64898/2026.11.16.767162
Publication status:
Preprint, v1

MetaboRx combines feature-table checks, QC metrics, visualization and preprocessing in an R Shiny workflow. The demonstration uses one semi-targeted mouse-plasma dataset with simulated quality defects rather than an external comparison; inspection of selected source code also shows that features with undefined QC relative standard deviation are removed at that filtering step even when the RSD cutoff is disabled. That behavior was not tested by running the software.

Reading scope: Deposited abstract and selected source code
