October 22, 2026 Literature Brief

Latest2026-10-22 · Beijing
English中文

Today's three papers examine how data choices shape computational mass spectrometry: structural benchmark partitions, sample-level proteomics quality control, and cross-study metabolomics integration.

The check also covered accessible listings and indexed records for lipidomics, imaging, ion mobility, DDA/DIA and modified peptides. Some ACS and MCP online-first pages were inaccessible, and other listings may lag publication; coverage is not exhaustive.

Critical Mass: Structure-Disjoint Splits Drift With the Libraries They Are Built On

Tomo Oga; Devesh Shah; Cailum M. K. Stienstra; Gabriel Asher; Antonio H. de O. Fonseca; Niall O'Connor; Michael Widrich

arXiv · 2026

First online:
2026-10-08
DOI:
10.48550/arXiv.2610.12694
Publication status:
Preprint, v1

Admissible bounds and union-find skipping reduce the integer programs needed for an unchanged structure-disjoint partition from about 26 billion to 11.5 million. At 227,690 molecules, MCES threshold 10 puts 95.5% in one component, exposing a benchmark bias that mixes structural novelty with extrapolation to larger molecules. This informs evaluation design, but remains specific to the libraries and threshold studied.

MCOD: a memory-constrained deep learning framework for robust outlier detection in quantitative proteomics

Jinze Huang; Huanyue Liao; Bo Meng; Guangkui Fan; Dong An; Xinhua Dai; Xiang Fang; Yang Zhao

Briefings in Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-09-26
DOI:
10.1093/bib/bbag507
Publication status:
Journal article

MCOD constrains an autoencoder using historical latent representations and stability-dependent weights. Tests compare 18 baselines on simulated HeLa perturbations and examine two cancer DDA cohorts. The regularization design is useful, but independent anomaly labels, imputation sensitivity and consistent group handling need further evaluation; generalization to DIA and phosphoproteomics is unproven.

Reading scope: Abstract and selected Methods, Results and Discussion sections; no reproduction

Metabolomics workbench: data-driven classification and correlation analysis between metabolite classes, biomarkers, and drugs

Mikhail M Khandokhin; Aleksandr Sokolov; Nikolay Oskolkov; Robert Fredriksson; Olga E Titova; Helgi B Schiöth

Briefings in Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-10-19
DOI:
10.1093/bib/bbag576
Publication status:
Journal article

The framework harmonizes metabolite names and combines structural embeddings with study-level rank statistics across 182 blood-metabolomics studies, 44,165 samples and 6,752 metabolites. It provides a reusable route for integrating public MS-derived data, not uniform reprocessing of raw spectra. Study quality, annotation and measurement heterogeneity remain limitations, and associations are not causal.

Reading scope: Publisher-indexed abstract and selected Methods, Results and Discussion excerpts; no reproduction
