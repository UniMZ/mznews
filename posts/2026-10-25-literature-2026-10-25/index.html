Literature briefing: 25 October 2026

Latest2026-10-25 · Beijing
English中文

Reliable quantitative proteomics depends on the entire analysis pipeline, not just a search engine. Today's selection examines the interaction between identification and downstream statistical tools.

The review also covered metabolomics, spectral searching, ion mobility and spatial metabolomics. Some recent candidates could not be included because original records or complete author information remained unverified; coverage is not exhaustive.

Comparison Study of Data Processing Workflows for Label-Free DIA Quantitative Proteomics

Valeriy I. Postoenko; Mikhail V. Gorshkov; Mark V. Ivanov

Journal of Proteome Research · 2026

First online:
2026-10-21
DOI:
10.1021/acs.jproteome.6c00778
Publication status:
Peer-reviewed article

The study combines four DIA search workflows (DIA-NN, DIA-Umpire, diaTracer and MSFragger-DIA) with four quantitative-analysis tools (directLFQ, AlphaPeptStats, Triqler and a simple t test), evaluating identification counts, differential-detection sensitivity, fold-change accuracy and quantitative false-discovery-rate control on two spike-in benchmarks. Its practical value is to assess search and downstream statistics as a coupled pipeline rather than select tools by identification count alone; the abstract does not disclose winning combinations or effect sizes, and generalization from two spike-in datasets to heterogeneous real samples requires full-text assessment.

Reading scope: Publisher abstract and indexed metadata; full text not read and results not reproduced.
