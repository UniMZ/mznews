Literature digest · October 16, 2026

Latest2026-10-16 · Beijing
English中文

This edition focuses on continuous QC for large proteomics cohorts, quantitative benchmarks with defined microbial communities, and interactive downstream analysis.

The priority is to connect continuous QC with benchmarks that measure quantitative accuracy as well as identification depth. Several journal sources were inaccessible, so coverage is incomplete.

LAMPrEY: a Python-Based Automated Quality Control Tool for Large-Scale Proteomics Datasets

Mario E. Valdés-Tresanco; Soren Wacker; Mario S. Valdés-Tresanco; Andriy Plakhotnyk; Nicholas I. Brodie; Morgan Hepburn; Annegret Ulke-Lemée; Edward L. Huttlin; Ian A. Lewis

Analytical Chemistry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-14
DOI:
10.1021/acs.analchem.6c02944
Publication status:
Version of record; earlier May 2026 preprint

LAMPrEY integrates MaxQuant, RawTools, interactive QC dashboards and an API into a traceable processing workflow for large TMT cohorts. The authors demonstrate longitudinal monitoring with TMT11 data from 910 Enterococcus faecium isolates, offering a practical design for embedding QC in continuously operating analysis pipelines. The abstract and software documentation do not establish anomaly-detection error rates, cross-instrument performance or gains over other QC tools.

Reading scope: Reviewed the publisher-deposited Crossref abstract, full authorship and online-date metadata, plus the official software homepage; publisher full text was inaccessible, and supplements and code were not audited or reproduced.

Asymmetric Track Lossless Analyzer Mass Spectrometry Improves Metaproteomic Analysis of a Complex Taxonomic Ground-Truth Microbial Community

Nicole M. Nightingale; Jana Richter; Nicolas Hartel; Mak A. Saito; Manuel Kleiner; Timothy J. Griffin; Pratik D. Jagtap

Journal of the American Society for Mass Spectrometry · 2026

First online:
2026-10-14
DOI:
10.1021/jasms.6c00283
Publication status:
Version of record

A defined community of 30 organisms spanning a wide abundance range benchmarks Astral against Orbitrap Fusion metaproteomics, including FAIMS trade-offs. The authors report that FAIMS increases peptide identifications while reducing precision and throughput, underscoring why known-composition benchmarks should evaluate quantification and taxonomic assignment rather than identification counts alone. Only the abstract was reviewed: numerical gains, search settings and error control remain unchecked, and instrument-vendor coauthors mean this is not a fully independent evaluation.

Reading scope: Reviewed the publisher abstract, byline, online date and supplement description; main text and supplementary benchmarks were not reviewed or reproduced.

Manchester Proteome Profiler: A User-Friendly Platform for Quantitative Proteomic Analysis

Stuart A. Cain; Mahak Fatima; Martin J. Humphries

Journal of Proteome Research · 2026

First online:
2026-10-15
DOI:
10.1021/acs.jproteome.5c01140
Publication status:
Version of record; earlier preprint posted 18 May 2026

Manchester Proteome Profiler combines protein-intensity filtering, normalization, imputation, differential analysis and clustering in an R Shiny interface, with SAINTexpress support for affinity-purification and proximity-labeling experiments. A KRAS proximity-biotinylation example illustrates reproducible protein clusters and a useful multi-input interactive workflow. The abstract does not provide systematic tests of missingness assumptions, statistical error control or competing pipelines, so it does not establish more accurate imputation or differential analysis.

Reading scope: Reviewed the publisher-deposited Crossref abstract, full authorship and online-date metadata, plus the authors’ institutional preprint record; publisher full text was inaccessible, and supplements and code were not audited or reproduced.
