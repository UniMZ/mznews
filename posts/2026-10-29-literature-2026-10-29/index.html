Literature Brief (October 29, 2026)

Latest2026-10-29 · Beijing
English中文

Today’s papers address interoperability in mass spectrometry imaging and the limits of transferring computational methods between proteomics and metabolomics. Shared data representations can reduce integration work, while identification confidence and quantification still depend on each field’s evidence structure.

This check covered accessible recent listings or indexes across Nature journals, ACS, MCP, Bioinformatics, PROTEOMICS, Genome Biology, PLOS Computational Biology and Metabolomics, with candidate papers checked against original sources. Access restrictions affected ACS, MCP and some preprints; candidates with incomplete authorship or scientific evidence were not included. Coverage is not exhaustive.

Thyra: bridging mass spectrometry imaging and SpatialData for unified multimodal analysis

Theodoros Visvikis; Tim F. E. Hendriks; Wouter-Michiel Vierdag; Luca Marconato; Ron M. A. Heeren; Eva Cuypers

Nature Methods · 2026

First online:
2026-10-28
DOI:
10.1038/s41592-026-03305-5
Publication status:
Version of record · Correspondence

Thyra converts continuous raw vendor data and imzML into SpatialData, connecting mass spectrometry imaging with histology, spatial transcriptomics and existing spatial-analysis tools; its documentation lists multiple instrument inputs and retention of available ion-mobility and MS/MS information. Example data and a tutorial support reuse, with the main value being a shared data representation rather than a new identification algorithm. The accessible preview does not establish cross-platform performance or registration accuracy, and an earlier JASMS methods paper already described the software.

Reading scope: Publisher article preview, author/publication metadata and official software documentation; full article not read and software not reproduced.

Data processing in proteomics and metabolomics: liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry-based omics with divergent computational paradigms

Amy Zheng; Bin Liao; Liang Zhang; Tao Huan

Briefings in Bioinformatics · 2026

First online:
2026-10-26
DOI:
10.1093/bib/bbag591
Publication status:
Published review

This review compares proteomics and metabolomics at the level of feature extraction, identification confidence and quantification, explaining how sequence constraints versus open chemical space shape computational workflows. It offers a useful framework for adapting methods across fields, but generates no new data and supplies neither a new algorithm nor independent benchmarking evidence.

Reading scope: Abstract and publisher-indexed main-text excerpts; not complete PDF or reproduction
